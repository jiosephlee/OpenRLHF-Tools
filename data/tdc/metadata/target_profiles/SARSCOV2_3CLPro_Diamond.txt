SARS-CoV-2 3CL protease (Mpro) is a cysteine protease essential for viral replication. It cleaves the viral polyprotein at 11 sites, making it a validated drug target. This dataset comes from a crystallographic fragment screen conducted at the Diamond Light Source, where fragments were soaked into Mpro crystals at high concentration (typically 50-200 mM) and binding was detected by electron density in difference maps. About 8% of screened fragments showed binding. High aqueous solubility is important for achieving sufficient soaking concentration — poorly soluble fragments may fail to produce detectable electron density even if they could bind.

The active site lies in a groove between domains I and II. The catalytic dyad consists of Cys145 (nucleophile) and His41 (general base). The oxyanion hole is formed by backbone NHs of Gly143, Ser144, and Cys145 — these stabilize the tetrahedral intermediate during substrate cleavage and also form hydrogen bonds with fragment carbonyl or sulfonyl groups. Key subsites: S1 (His163, Glu166/Phe140 backbones — prefers small H-bond acceptors like lactams, pyridones, or pyrazoles), S2 (His41, Met49, Met165 — a hydrophobic pocket that accommodates medium-sized aromatics and aliphatic groups), S1' (shallow and solvent-exposed — tolerates diverse substituents), and S4 (Gln189, flexible and partially solvent-exposed). The combined S1+S2 pocket volume is roughly 300 Å³.

About 40% of confirmed fragment hits bind covalently. Electrophilic warheads react with the Cys145 thiolate: chloroacetamides are the most common (SN2 displacement of chloride), followed by acrylamides (Michael addition) and vinyl sulfonamides. The remaining ~60% bind non-covalently through H-bond networks with the oxyanion hole and S1 pocket residues, plus hydrophobic packing against Met49 and Met165 in S2.

Favorable molecular features for binding: an electrophilic warhead capable of reacting with Cys145, H-bond donor/acceptor pairs complementary to the oxyanion hole (e.g., amides, sulfonamides, heterocyclic NHs), compact size (MW < 300 Da for fragment-level crystallographic detection), high aqueous solubility (logS > -2), and some three-dimensional character to complement the binding groove shape. Unfavorable features: bulky groups that exceed the subsite volume, purely hydrophobic molecules lacking H-bond capacity, heavy halogens like iodine (whose strong anomalous X-ray scattering can complicate electron density interpretation and lead to false negatives), and highly flexible molecules with many rotatable bonds (entropic penalty for adopting a bound conformation).
