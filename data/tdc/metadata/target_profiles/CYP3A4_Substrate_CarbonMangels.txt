CYP3A4 is the most abundant cytochrome P450 enzyme in the human liver (30-40% of hepatic CYP content) and intestinal epithelium. It metabolizes approximately 50% of all marketed drugs, making it the single most important drug-metabolizing enzyme. Its broad substrate specificity stems from an exceptionally large and flexible active site cavity (~1400 Å³) that can accommodate bulky, structurally diverse molecules — and can even bind two substrates simultaneously (cooperative binding).

The CYP3A4 active site is a large, primarily hydrophobic chamber with considerable conformational flexibility. Unlike CYP2D6 (which requires a basic nitrogen) or CYP2C9 (which prefers acidic substrates), CYP3A4 has no strong electrostatic selectivity — it accommodates neutral, basic, and acidic compounds. The heme iron sits at the bottom, and substrates can adopt multiple binding orientations, often leading to metabolism at several sites on the same molecule. Key residues include Phe304 and Ile369, which form hydrophobic contacts.

Molecular features associated with being a CYP3A4 substrate: larger molecular size (MW > 300 Da, often > 400 Da) — the large active site preferentially accommodates bulky molecules. High lipophilicity (logP > 2-3) facilitates partitioning into the hydrophobic cavity. Multiple aromatic rings or extended hydrophobic surfaces. The presence of oxidizable functional groups (N-dealkylation sites, aromatic hydroxylation sites, aliphatic C-H bonds adjacent to heteroatoms). Structural flexibility — molecules with multiple rotatable bonds can adopt conformations that fit the cavity. Many CYP3A4 substrates contain a basic nitrogen, but unlike CYP2D6, this is not a strict requirement.

Features arguing against CYP3A4 metabolism: small molecular size (MW < 200 Da) — small molecules may not have sufficient contact surface for productive binding in the large cavity. Very hydrophilic compounds (logP < 0) that cannot partition into the active site. Metabolically stable scaffolds — fully substituted aromatics, fluorinated positions, sterically shielded metabolic sites. Very rigid, compact molecules with minimal hydrophobic surface area.
