CYP2C9 is a cytochrome P450 enzyme predominantly expressed in the liver, responsible for the oxidative metabolism of approximately 15% of clinically used drugs. It preferentially metabolizes weakly acidic compounds with a hydrogen bond acceptor located 5-7 Å from the site of oxidation. Important CYP2C9 substrates include warfarin, phenytoin, losartan, tolbutamide, and many NSAIDs (diclofenac, ibuprofen, celecoxib). CYP2C9 is highly polymorphic — the *2 and *3 alleles have substantially reduced activity, leading to clinically significant pharmacogenomic variability.

The CYP2C9 active site is a relatively small, hydrophobic cavity with a negatively charged region near the entrance that preferentially accommodates anionic or weakly acidic substrates. The heme iron at the base of the cavity performs the oxidation. Substrates bind with the site of metabolism positioned close to the reactive ferryl-oxo species, while an acidic functional group (carboxylate, acidic NH) interacts with Arg108 near the active site entrance.

Molecular features associated with being a CYP2C9 substrate: the presence of an acidic or weakly acidic group (carboxylic acid, phenol, sulfonamide, acidic heterocycle like tetrazole) that can form an ionic or H-bond interaction with Arg108 — this is the most distinctive feature of CYP2C9 substrates compared to other CYPs. Moderate lipophilicity (logP 1-4) for partitioning into the hydrophobic active site. A metabolically labile site (aromatic ring, benzylic position, allylic position, or heteroatom with available lone pairs) positioned approximately 5-7 Å from the acidic moiety. Molecular weight typically 200-500 Da. Planar or semi-planar aromatic scaffolds fit the active site well.

Features arguing against CYP2C9 metabolism: absence of any acidic functional group — strongly basic or permanently cationic compounds are poor CYP2C9 substrates (they are more likely CYP2D6 substrates). Very bulky or highly branched structures that cannot fit the relatively compact active site. Very small molecules (MW < 150) may not have sufficient binding affinity. Molecules lacking any oxidizable site near the required distance from an anchoring group.
