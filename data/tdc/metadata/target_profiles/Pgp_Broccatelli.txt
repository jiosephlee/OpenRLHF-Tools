P-glycoprotein (Pgp, also called MDR1 or ABCB1) is an ATP-binding cassette (ABC) efflux transporter expressed at the apical membrane of intestinal enterocytes, the blood-brain barrier endothelium, renal proximal tubules, and hepatic canalicular membranes. It actively pumps substrates from the intracellular space back into the lumen/blood, reducing oral absorption, brain penetration, and increasing renal and biliary clearance. Inhibition of Pgp can dramatically alter a co-administered drug's pharmacokinetics and tissue distribution. This dataset predicts whether a compound inhibits Pgp.

Pgp has a large, flexible drug-binding pocket within the transmembrane domain that can accommodate structurally diverse substrates and inhibitors. The binding cavity is predominantly hydrophobic, lined by aromatic and aliphatic residues from transmembrane helices TM6 and TM12. It can adopt multiple conformations to accommodate different molecular shapes — this explains Pgp's unusually broad substrate specificity.

Molecular features associated with Pgp inhibition: high lipophilicity (logP > 3) is strongly associated with Pgp inhibition, as hydrophobic molecules partition into the membrane and access the transmembrane binding site. Molecular weight > 400 Da — larger molecules are more likely to inhibit Pgp. Multiple aromatic rings (≥2) provide hydrophobic surface area and van der Waals contacts with the binding cavity. The presence of hydrogen bond acceptors (particularly tertiary amines, ethers, esters) facilitates interactions with polar residues at the edges of the binding pocket. Tertiary or secondary amines (often basic) are common in Pgp inhibitors.

Features associated with non-inhibitors: low lipophilicity (logP < 2) — highly polar, hydrophilic molecules do not partition into the membrane to reach the binding site. Small molecular weight (< 300 Da) — compact molecules tend not to fill the large binding cavity effectively. Few aromatic rings (0-1). High TPSA (> 120 Å²) and many hydrogen bond donors make molecules too polar for membrane partitioning. Fully charged species (strong acids, quaternary ammonium) at physiological pH are generally poor Pgp inhibitors.
