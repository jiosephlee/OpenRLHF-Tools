ClinTox is a dataset of drugs that have been approved by the FDA or failed in clinical trials due to toxicity. It captures a broad range of clinical toxicity outcomes — hepatotoxicity, cardiotoxicity, nephrotoxicity, neurotoxicity, severe hypersensitivity, and other organ-specific or systemic adverse effects that led to clinical failure, black box warnings, or market withdrawal. Unlike single-endpoint assays (e.g., hERG for cardiotoxicity, DILI for liver toxicity), ClinTox aggregates multiple toxicity mechanisms, making it a more complex prediction task.

Clinical toxicity can arise from on-target effects (exaggerated pharmacology at the intended target), off-target effects (unintended activity at other biological targets), or reactive metabolite-mediated toxicity (bioactivation to chemically reactive species that damage proteins, DNA, or lipids). Many clinical toxicity failures involve idiosyncratic reactions — rare, unpredictable responses in susceptible individuals that are difficult to detect in standard preclinical studies.

Structural features associated with clinical toxicity: reactive functional groups that can form covalent adducts with proteins (quinone/quinone imine precursors from anilines and aminophenols, thiophene S-oxidation, furan ring opening to cis-enedials, epoxides from alkene bioactivation, acyl glucuronide formation from carboxylic acids). Aniline and para-aminophenol motifs — historically over-represented among withdrawn drugs due to reactive metabolite formation. Structural alerts shared with DILI: nitroaromatics, hydrazines, and Michael acceptors. High daily dose (>100 mg/day) is a strong risk factor for idiosyncratic toxicity. High lipophilicity (logP > 3) combined with extensive hepatic metabolism increases exposure to reactive intermediates.

Features associated with clinical safety: metabolically stable scaffolds with few bioactivation liabilities. Low daily dose requirement (<10 mg/day). Absence of known toxicophores. Selective pharmacology with limited off-target activity. Good physicochemical properties (moderate logP, reasonable MW) that avoid excessive tissue accumulation.
