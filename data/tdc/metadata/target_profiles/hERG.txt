The human ether-à-go-go related gene (hERG) encodes the Kv11.1 voltage-gated potassium channel, which conducts the rapid delayed rectifier current (IKr) essential for cardiac repolarization. Drug-induced hERG blockade prolongs the QT interval and can trigger fatal torsades de pointes arrhythmia. hERG is notoriously promiscuous — its inner cavity is unusually large and lipophilic compared to other potassium channels, making it a frequent off-target for diverse drug scaffolds.

The primary binding site is the inner cavity of the channel pore, accessible from the intracellular side when the channel is open. Two aromatic residues are critical: Tyr652 and Phe656 on the S6 helix, which line the cavity and form cation-π and π-stacking interactions with drug molecules. The cavity is roughly 10-12 Å in diameter and can accommodate molecules with extended conformations. Additional contacts involve Thr623, Ser624, and Val625 at the base of the selectivity filter. Unlike most K+ channels, hERG lacks the Pro-Val-Pro motif in S6, creating a wider, more drug-accessible cavity.

The dominant molecular recognition mechanism is cation-π interaction: a protonated (cationic) basic amine in the drug interacts with the aromatic π-systems of Tyr652 and Phe656. This is why a basic nitrogen with pKa well above physiological pH (so that it is predominantly protonated at pH 7.4) is the single most important feature of hERG blockers. Molecules with multiple aromatic rings can additionally engage in π-stacking with these residues, and lipophilic character promotes partitioning into the hydrophobic channel cavity.

Features that increase hERG blocking risk: a basic nitrogen with high pKa (piperidine pKa ~10.5, piperazine pKa ~9 — both strongly associated with hERG liability), high lipophilicity (logP > 3 is a commonly cited risk factor, though this is a trend rather than a hard cutoff), multiple aromatic or planar ring systems capable of π-stacking, and an extended molecular shape that spans the distance between Tyr652/Phe656 pairs on opposing S6 helices. Features that reduce hERG risk: weakly basic or non-basic nitrogen atoms (morpholine pKa ~6.5 is a classic hERG-sparing replacement for piperidine — only ~12% protonated at pH 7.4, dramatically reducing cation-π engagement), low lipophilicity (logP < 2), high polar surface area (TPSA > 75 Å²), absence of aromatic rings or the presence of only one small aromatic ring, and overall compactness that prevents spanning the cavity. Molecules with no basic nitrogen at all very rarely block hERG, because the critical cation-π interaction cannot form.
