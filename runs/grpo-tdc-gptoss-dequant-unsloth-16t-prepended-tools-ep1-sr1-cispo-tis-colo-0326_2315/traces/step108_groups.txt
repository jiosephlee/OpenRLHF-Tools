Context: The Parallel Artificial Membrane Permeability Assay (PAMPA) measures passive transcellular permeability by quantifying how much compound diffuses across an artificial lipid membrane (typically a phospholipid mixture on a filter support) from a donor to an acceptor compartment. Unlike Caco-2 cell assays, PAMPA has no active transporters, no efflux pumps, and no paracellular pathway — it isolates pure passive diffusion. The NCATS variant uses a standardized protocol across a large compound library.

The rate of passive diffusion is governed by the drug's ability to partition from aqueous solution into the lipid bilayer, diffuse across it, and re-partition into the aqueous phase on the other side.
